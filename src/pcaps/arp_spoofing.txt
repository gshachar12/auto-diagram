Incident Report: ARP Cache Poisoning on 192.168.1.0/24
======================================================

Summary
-------
A host on the local network, 192.168.1.105, poisoned the ARP cache of the
workstation 192.168.1.104 so that the workstation sent its internet-bound
traffic through the attacker instead of the gateway. The capture lasts
543 seconds (16,285 packets, about 14.9 MB). The capture clock was not
set, so timestamps start at the Unix epoch; times below are seconds from
the start of the capture.

Hosts
-----
  Gateway:   192.168.1.1    MAC 08:00:27:5e:01:7c
  Victim:    192.168.1.104  MAC 08:00:27:b8:b7:58 (Linux, Firefox 60)
  Attacker:  192.168.1.105  MAC 08:00:27:2d:f8:5a

Normal activity (0 - 480 s)
---------------------------
The capture opens at 17.5 s with DHCP and IPv6 neighbour discovery as the
machines came up. The victim then browsed the web. It resolved names
through the gateway at 192.168.1.1 over DNS, made plain-HTTP OCSP and
captive-portal checks, and opened HTTPS sessions to Reddit's content
delivery network (151.101.x.140) and to Google and Mozilla services. From
about 387 s the Reddit sessions on port 443 delivered roughly 14 MB to the
victim. Every minute or two the victim sent an ARP request for
192.168.1.1 and the genuine gateway MAC answered.

Poisoning (481 - 533 s)
-----------------------
At 481 s the attacker broadcast an ARP request for 192.168.1.104 and the
victim replied. Starting at 482 s the attacker sent unsolicited ARP
replies to the victim every two seconds, 26 in total until 532 s, each
claiming that 192.168.1.1 is at the attacker's MAC 08:00:27:2d:f8:5a.

Interception (505 - 534 s)
--------------------------
From 505 s the victim addressed its internet-bound frames to the
attacker's MAC: 2,191 frames that should have gone to the gateway went to
the attacker instead. Return traffic from the internet still came from
the real gateway MAC directly to the victim, so only the outbound
direction was intercepted. While forwarding, the attacker sent nine ICMP
redirect messages (type 5, code 1) to the victim between 505 s and 522 s
pointing it back at 192.168.1.1.

Recovery (534 - 539 s)
----------------------
At 534 s five ARP replies, about one per second, mapped 192.168.1.1 back
to its genuine MAC 08:00:27:5e:01:7c and the victim's traffic returned to
the gateway.

Assessment
----------
The pattern matches tools such as arpspoof or Ettercap run from a Kali
machine. Because the victim's sessions were TLS-encrypted, the attacker
could see destinations and volumes but probably not page contents. We
recommend dynamic ARP inspection on the access switch and static ARP
entries for the gateway on sensitive hosts.
