Incident Report: Spoofed SYN Flood against a Minecraft Server
=============================================================

Summary
-------
On 2021-04-28 at 13:30:21 UTC the game server 10.10.10.10 received a TCP
SYN flood aimed at its Minecraft port 25565. In 23.7 seconds the sensor
recorded 37,841 SYN packets from 37,623 different source addresses. The
customer reported that players were disconnected during the attack.

Attack traffic
--------------
Every packet in the capture is a bare TCP SYN to 10.10.10.10 port 25565.
The source addresses are scattered across the whole IPv4 space and almost
never repeat: 37,405 addresses sent exactly one SYN and only 218 sent
two. Source ports are random. The packets carry a TCP window of 0, no
TCP options and no payload, and arrive with a TTL of 242 or 244 whatever
their claimed origin. Genuine clients from thousands of networks would
show varied TTLs and normal TCP options, so the sources are almost
certainly spoofed by a single flooding tool or booter service.

Timeline
--------
The attack came in three phases. It opened with a burst of 23,620 SYNs
in the first second and 675 in the next. After a short pause a second
burst delivered 10,193 SYNs in the fourth second and 2,551 in the fifth.
The sender then went quiet for about eight seconds and resumed at a low,
steady rate of 60 to 95 SYNs per second from 13 seconds until the end of
the capture.

Victim response
---------------
The capture contains no SYN-ACK or RST packets from 10.10.10.10. Either
the server's backlog was already full and it stopped answering, or the
sensor only mirrors inbound traffic.

Assessment
----------
Minecraft servers are a common target for paid DDoS services, often
after in-game disputes. We recommend enabling SYN cookies on the server
and asking the hosting provider for upstream filtering of spoofed SYNs.
