Incident Report: TCP SYN Port Scan against 10.100.18.12
=======================================================

Summary
-------
The internal host 10.100.25.14 probed 29 well-known TCP ports on the
server 10.100.18.12 on 2006-08-24 at 17:35:25 UTC. The scan lasted 2.8
seconds. It looks like the reconnaissance stage before a targeted
intrusion, most likely an Nmap-style half-open scan.

Scan traffic
------------
The scanner sent exactly one TCP SYN packet to each port, 29 SYNs in
total, roughly every 100 milliseconds. It started with the Windows
networking ports 139 (NetBIOS), 135 (RPC) and 445 (SMB), then moved on to
80 (HTTP), 22 (SSH), 515 (printer), 23 (Telnet), 21 (FTP) and 6000 (X11),
and continued with 1025, 25, 111, 1028, 9100, 1029, 79, 497, 548, 5000,
1917, 53, 161, 9001, 65535, 443, 113, 993, 8080 and 2869. No port was
probed twice.

Every SYN came from a different random source port, had no payload, a
TTL of 64 and an unusually small TCP window of 8 bytes. A real operating
system never opens connections with such a window, which shows that the
packets were crafted by a scanning tool.

Responses
---------
No packets from 10.100.18.12 back to the scanner appear in the capture:
no SYN-ACKs from open ports and no RSTs from closed ports. Either a
host firewall dropped the probes silently or the sensor only saw one
direction of the traffic.

Assessment
----------
The choice of ports suggests the attacker was looking for file-sharing,
remote administration and printer services. The scanning host should be
investigated for compromise, and the server's firewall logs checked for
follow-up connection attempts.
